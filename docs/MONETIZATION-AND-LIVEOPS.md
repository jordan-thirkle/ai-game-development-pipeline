# Monetisation, Growth, and Live Operations

The factory optimises for sustainable player value and sustainable economics together. Monetisation is a product-system decision, not a bolt-on added immediately before release.

## Order of operations

1. Prove the player fantasy and core loop.
2. Prove repeated enjoyment/return reasons.
3. Understand session structure and player segments.
4. Identify monetisation models that naturally fit the experience.
5. Prototype and test the least invasive viable model.
6. Instrument economics and player impact.
7. Expand only when both product health and economics justify it.

## Monetisation candidates

Evaluate rather than assume:

- premium purchase;
- free with cosmetic/content IAP;
- expansion/DLC/content packs;
- rewarded advertising;
- limited non-rewarded advertising where genre expectations support it;
- subscription only when recurring value genuinely exists;
- supporter/founder purchases;
- platform-specific opportunities;
- no direct monetisation when strategic audience/research value is the stronger goal.

Hybrid models require explicit complexity justification.

## Required decision factors

For every monetisation model score:

- player/genre fit;
- effect on retention and trust;
- expected conversion/revenue potential;
- implementation complexity;
- QA matrix;
- store/platform dependency;
- support/refund/fraud burden;
- analytics requirements;
- content obligations;
- operational cost;
- maintenance/migration risk;
- localisation/regional implications;
- age/audience implications;
- brand impact.

Do not optimise short-term revenue at the expense of the game's reason to exist.

## Unit economics

Track costs that are easy to hide during AI-assisted development:

- model inference/API usage;
- asset generation;
- build minutes/CI;
- hosting/CDN;
- databases/storage;
- multiplayer/realtime services;
- analytics/logging;
- email/push;
- customer support;
- paid assets/licences;
- store/platform fees;
- acquisition spend;
- refunds/chargebacks;
- moderation/anti-abuse;
- recurring human intervention.

The goal is not zero cost. The goal is the highest long-term leverage and margin for the intended product.

## Experiment design

A monetisation experiment must define before release:

- hypothesis;
- target cohort;
- primary player metric;
- primary business metric;
- guardrail metrics;
- minimum evidence threshold;
- rollout size;
- rollback criteria;
- expected operational cost;
- post-test keep/revert/iterate decision.

Do not interpret higher purchase/ad metrics as success if retention, reviews, session quality, or trust deteriorate materially.

## Growth and distribution

Treat distribution as part of product design.

Evaluate:
- App Store / Play Store discovery;
- web/PWA where applicable;
- games.byjtt.com organic search and internal linking;
- playable demos;
- creator/streamer suitability;
- shareable moments;
- social clips/screenshots;
- community loops;
- platform featuring opportunities;
- cross-promotion among By JTT games;
- developer/research audience generated by the public lab.

Do not manufacture features solely for virality if they harm the game.

## LiveOps

LiveOps must earn its operational complexity.

Prefer systems where agents can safely create validated content from data rather than code:
- encounter definitions;
- rotations;
- challenges;
- events;
- rewards;
- balance tables;
- quests;
- cosmetics;
- announcements;
- remote configuration.

All remote/content changes need validation, versioning, rollback, and observability.

## Content economics

For each repeatable content type measure:

- agent effort;
- human intervention;
- generation cost;
- validation/rejection rate;
- art/animation/audio production cost;
- regression risk;
- player engagement;
- retention/revenue impact;
- expected useful lifetime.

A content pipeline is successful when valuable new content becomes cheaper and safer to ship over time.

## Pricing and economy integrity

Where a virtual economy exists:
- model sources/sinks;
- prevent accidental inflation/deflation;
- make purchases/restores idempotent;
- record authoritative transaction state where needed;
- test offline/retry/failure cases;
- maintain reconciliation capability;
- separate balance configuration from transaction correctness.

Never let generated content or AI agents directly mutate production economic state without bounded permissions, validation, and rollback.

## Post-launch cadence

Cadence follows evidence rather than an arbitrary promise.

A healthy update loop asks:
1. What is currently limiting player value or sustainable growth?
2. What evidence supports that diagnosis?
3. Is the strongest response a bug fix, balance change, content addition, UX improvement, technical migration, distribution change, monetisation experiment, or no change?
4. Can the response be tested at small scope?
5. What would make us revert it?

## Long-term rule

Revenue is evidence that players value some aspect of the product, not permission to stop improving it. A commercially successful game enters a stricter maintenance and experimentation loop because more players and more money increase the cost of mistakes.
